# EzEval — Product & Technical Specification

**Purpose of this document:** This is the authoritative build spec. Open decisions are marked **[OPEN]** and must be resolved or defaulted before implementation of the affected area.

## 1. Product Overview

EzEval is a SaaS quoting tool for window cleaning businesses (and, by design, adaptable to similar field-service trades). It replaces the founder's original 2021 personal tool — a fixed price sheet that was tapped through on-site, with the final dollar amount copied onto paper and re-entered later — with a multi-tenant, subscription-based web application.

The core product loop:

1. A business owner signs up, subscribes ($5/month via Stripe), and configures one or more **price sheets**.
2. Estimators (or technicians, or the owner) open a price sheet on-site, tap buttons corresponding to windows/items by access level, and the app builds an itemized evaluation.
3. The evaluation captures the metrics (item type, height/level, size, count) and computes a total — with configurable controls over who can see prices.
4. A lightweight task/CRM layer schedules estimation runs, optimizes routes, and enforces per-business workflows (e.g., "call the customer before arriving").

### Why it exists (problem statement)

Field estimation in window cleaning is currently ad-hoc: paper notes, memorized price sheets, owner-only pricing knowledge. This creates three problems the product solves directly:

- **Inefficiency:** quotes are double-handled (tapped on-site, transcribed at home).
- **Pricing IP leakage risk:** giving a full price sheet to a $15/hour estimator means they could use it to compete with the owner. EzEval lets owners deploy estimators who *collect metrics only*, or see only a grand total, never per-button prices.
- **Inconsistent field workflow:** owners have no way to ensure estimators follow the intended process (pre-arrival calls, on-site notes, etc.).

### Business model

- Simple subscription: **$5/month per business** (launch pricing), billed through Stripe.
- If a paid mapping provider is required (e.g., Mapbox), its cost is factored into the monthly price rather than passed through as a separate fee.

## 3. Users, Roles & Permissions

Three roles, all scoped to a business account:

| Role | Description | Key permissions |
|---|---|---|
| **Admin** (business owner) | Creates the account, manages billing, configures everything. | Full access: sees all prices, creates/edits price sheets, manages users, manages business info, manages customers/tasks. |
| **Estimator** | Field worker sent out to evaluate jobs. May be low-wage staff the owner does not want to expose pricing to. | Uses price sheets to build evaluations. **Price visibility is restricted and configurable per price sheet** (see 4.5). Receives assigned tasks/runs. |
| **Technician / Window cleaner** | Primarily does the cleaning work, but occasionally needs to build an on-site quote (e.g., a neighbor at a job site asks for one). | Can open price sheets and capture an evaluation on the spot, subject to the same price-visibility rules as estimators. |

**Design principle:** *Empower everybody to capture quote information the moment the opportunity appears* — without leaking the owner's pricing model. The permission system exists to let an owner hand the tool to a $15/hour estimator confident that the estimator cannot reconstruct the price sheet and use it to compete.

### Account & business setup

- Business owners self-serve: create an account, attach a credit card, and are billed $5/month via Stripe.
- During/after onboarding, owners can enter **business information** (name, contact details, etc.). This is stored now primarily so it is ready for the future invoicing feature (Section 8) — invoices and outbound quotes will be pre-populated from it.

## 4. Price Sheets (Core Feature)

### 4.1 Concept

A price sheet is a configurable grid of buttons that an estimator taps while walking a property. Each button represents a billable item at a given access complexity. Tapping buttons accumulates line items into an evaluation.

### 4.2 Default layout (founder's reference sheet)

The default price sheet shipped to every new business mirrors the founder's original tool — four rows organized by **access level**, where "level" means the complexity/labor involved in reaching the glass:

| Row | Meaning | Pricing implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Special** | Non-standard items: setup fee, French panes, skylights, etc. | Item-specific (see below). |
| **High level** | Roof access — climbing up, setting a rope anchor, roping down to the window. Detailed and labor-intensive. | Highest prices. |
| **Mid level** | Reachable with a water-fed pole. | Moderate prices. |
| **Ground level** | Walk up to the window and clean it. | Lowest prices. |

Special items, in detail:

- **Setup fee:** a minimum chunk billed on every job. It covers overhead that exists regardless of job size — advertising, doing estimates, getting "boots on the ground." See 4.4 for its toggle behavior.
- **French panes:** many small individual panes; typically priced **per pane** (≈ $1/pane in the founder's practice).
- **Skylights:** their own special line item.

### 4.3 Configurability (admin-facing)

Admins can create and edit price sheets. A price sheet definition controls:

- **Grid dimensions:** how many rows and how many columns (i.e., how many buttons).
- **Button labels:** what every button says.
- **Button prices:** what every button costs.
- **Setup fee behavior:** whether a setup fee exists and whether it auto-attaches (4.4).
- **Price visibility & minimum total settings** (4.5).

Businesses will configure sheets in many different ways — this flexibility is an explicitly captured requirement, not a nice-to-have.

### 4.4 Multiple price sheets per business

A business can maintain **multiple, completely separate price sheets** — e.g., a *Residential* sheet and a *Commercial* sheet with different buttons and prices. When dispatching an estimator, the job type determines which sheet they use.

**Setup fee toggle:** configured per price sheet. Example intent: "if anybody creates an evaluation on the Residential window-cleaning sheet, the setup fee is charged no matter what and automatically attaches to the total." When enabled, the fee is added automatically — the estimator does not have to remember to tap it.

### 4.5 Evaluation output, price visibility & anti-leakage controls

**What an evaluation produces:** as buttons are tapped, the app generates an **itemized list capturing height (level), size, and count for each item** entered. The metrics are the primary capture; the dollar math happens on top of them.

**Price visibility is role- and sheet-dependent:**

- **Admins always see everything** — per-item prices and totals.
- **Estimators**, depending on a per-price-sheet toggle, operate in one of two modes:
  1. **Metrics-only mode:** the estimator collects the item counts/levels/sizes but sees **no dollar amounts at all**. The owner prices the job back at the office.
  2. **Grand-total mode:** the estimator can quote on-site, but sees **only the grand total at the end of the evaluation** — never per-button prices.

- **Minimum quote amount:** a per-sheet configurable floor (founder's example: **$150**). Its purpose is anti-reverse-engineering: without it, an estimator in grand-total mode could tap one button, read the total, and thereby learn that single button's price. With a floor, small evaluations all display the minimum, masking individual button values. (It also reflects real-world minimum job pricing.)

### 4.6 Acceptance criteria — price sheets

- [ ] New business accounts are provisioned with the default 4-row sheet (Special / High / Mid / Ground) described in 4.2.
- [ ] Admin can create additional price sheets; each is independent (own grid, labels, prices, toggles).
- [ ] Admin can set rows, columns, button labels, and button prices on any sheet.
- [ ] Per sheet, admin can enable an auto-attaching setup fee; when enabled, every evaluation on that sheet includes the fee in its total without user action.
- [ ] Per sheet, admin can set estimator price visibility: metrics-only or grand-total-only.
- [ ] Per sheet, admin can set a minimum quote amount; displayed totals never fall below it.
- [ ] An evaluation produces an itemized list of {item/button, level/height, size, count} regardless of whether prices are visible to the person capturing it.
- [ ] Given an estimator on a metrics-only sheet, when they complete an evaluation, then no dollar amounts are rendered anywhere in their UI.
- [ ] Given an estimator on a grand-total sheet with a $150 minimum, when they tap a single $12 button, then the displayed total is $150 and no per-item price is shown.
- [ ] Admin viewing the same evaluation sees full per-item pricing.

## 5. Tasks & Lightweight CRM

### 5.1 Concept

A task system layered on basic CRM data. It exists to organize estimation runs and enforce each business's field workflow.

### 5.2 CRM data

- **Customers** stored per business, with **address information** (and standard contact details).
- **Property notes**, critically including **animals on the property** — dogs, goats, horses, anything an estimator or technician should be aware of before walking onto a property. This is an explicit, first-class field on estimation jobs, not an afterthought.

### 5.3 Tasks, runs & assignment

- Admins assign estimators to a **run** — a set of estimation appointments.
- The task system surfaces "the next appointment" for the estimator: where to go, what to do next.
- **Scheduling semantics:**
  - Some customers want a quote at a **specific time** — those are fixed appointments.
  - Customers with no specified time are **worked into the daily schedule** flexibly.
  - Customers do **not** need to be home for an estimate, as long as they are aware the company will be on their property. The system should reflect/communicate this.

### 5.4 Configurable workflow steps

Each business defines its own workflow that estimators/technicians must follow per task. Canonical example: *the estimator must phone the customer before arrival* — "Hi John, my name is Ryan from \<company\>, I'll be at your place in 25 minutes to do your quote, and the quote will arrive in your inbox shortly after."

The intent: whatever workflow the business has set up, the system gives the owner confidence that field staff are **following the plan and communicating with customers** about when they'll be around. Workflow steps should be definable by admins and tracked/checked off per task.

### 5.5 Route mapping & optimization

- Integrate a map (Mapbox, or preferably a free provider — **[OPEN]**, see 2.1).
- **Intelligent route ordering:** given the day's assigned stops, order them so the estimator travels one efficient loop ("one big ring around town" — A → B → C → D) instead of criss-crossing back and forth.

### 5.6 Acceptance criteria — tasks/CRM

- [ ] Admin can create customers with address and contact info.
- [ ] Estimation jobs support an "animals on property" note that is prominently visible to the assigned field worker before/at arrival.
- [ ] Admin can assign a set of estimation tasks (a run) to a specific estimator.
- [ ] Estimator sees their next appointment, in route-optimized order.
- [ ] Tasks distinguish fixed-time appointments from flexible ones; fixed-time appointments anchor the schedule, flexible ones fill around them.
- [ ] Admin can define workflow steps (e.g., "call customer before arrival") that appear on each task for completion/check-off.
- [ ] Map view displays the day's stops and the optimized route.

## 7. Data Model (initial sketch)

To be refined, but the dictated requirements imply at least:

- **businesses** — name, business/contact info (pre-captured for future invoicing), Stripe customer/subscription refs.
- **users** — belongs to business; role ∈ {admin, estimator, technician}.
- **price_sheets** — belongs to business; name/type (e.g., Residential, Commercial); settings: setup-fee enabled + amount, estimator visibility mode (metrics-only | grand-total), minimum quote amount; grid dimensions.
- **price_sheet_buttons** — belongs to price sheet; row (level/category), column position, label, price, pricing unit (flat vs per-pane for items like French panes).
- **customers** — belongs to business; name, address, contact info.
- **tasks / appointments** — belongs to business; customer ref; assigned user; fixed time or flexible flag; status; animal/property notes; workflow-step checklist state.
- **workflow_templates / steps** — per business definition of required steps per task.
- **evaluations (quotes)** — belongs to business; customer/task refs; created-by user; line items of {button ref, level, size, count, unit price, line total}; setup fee applied; computed total; minimum applied flag.

## 8. Future Considerations (explicitly out of scope for v1, design for later)

- **Invoicing:** generate invoices/outbound quote documents from evaluations, pre-populated with the stored business information. Not the most important feature now, but firmly planned — which is *why* business info is collected at onboarding. Schema and evaluation structure should not preclude it.
- **Customer-facing quote delivery** ("the quote will come to your inbox shortly") — implied by the workflow example; emailing quotes to customers is a likely follow-on to invoicing.
